How Much Does It Cost to Paint a Room?

Painting an average bedroom costs $60–$150 in materials yourself, or $350–$900 to hire out. The gap is almost entirely labour — and painting is the single most approachable DIY job in a house.

What a Room Actually Costs

For a typical 12×12 bedroom with 8-foot ceilings — about 384 square feet of wall before openings — here is the honest breakdown.

Doing it yourself: two coats need roughly 2–3 gallons of paint at $30–$55 a gallon ($60–$165), plus primer if you are covering a dark colour or bare drywall ($25–$40), plus supplies — tape, drop cloths, roller covers, brushes, filler ($30–$60 the first time, far less afterwards). Call it $90–$250 all in for a first room, dropping toward $60–$150 once you own the tools.

Hiring a painter: $350–$900 for the same room, typically quoted at $2–$6 per square foot of floor area or $1.50–$4 per square foot of wall. Ceilings, trim and doors are usually priced separately — a full room including ceiling and trim can reach $700–$1,200.

Regional labour rates drive most of that variation, along with ceiling height and how much preparation the walls need.

How Much Paint You Actually Need

The rule of thumb is one gallon per 350–400 square feet per coat, and almost everyone buys too much or too little by ignoring the coat count.

For that 12×12 room: perimeter 48 feet × 8-foot ceiling = 384 sq ft of wall, minus about 21 sq ft per door and 15 per window — say 348 sq ft with one of each. At 375 sq ft per gallon that is 0.93 gallons per coat, so two gallons for two coats with a little spare. Add the ceiling (144 sq ft) and you want a third gallon of ceiling paint.

The paint calculator runs this from your dimensions, openings and coat count, and now prices it at whatever your paint costs. Two coats is not optional with a colour change — one coat over a different colour looks patchy in daylight no matter how good the paint is.

Where the Price Really Varies

Wall condition. This is the biggest swing in professional quotes. Smooth, sound walls paint fast. Walls with nail holes, cracks, water stains, peeling paint or old wallpaper adhesive need filling, sanding, spot-priming and sometimes skim-coating — adding hours that show up as hundreds of dollars.

Colour change. Going dark-to-light, or covering a saturated colour, can mean primer plus three coats. Going light-to-light is two coats and done.

Ceiling height. Eight-foot walls are rollers on poles. Vaulted or two-storey walls mean ladders, scaffolding and slower, more careful work — often a 50–100% premium per square foot.

Trim, doors and ceilings. Cutting in trim and doors is detail work measured in hours, not square feet. A room quoted cheaply may be walls only; always confirm what is included.

Paint grade. A $55 gallon genuinely covers better than a $25 one, often turning three coats into two. On labour-heavy jobs, better paint is the cheaper choice.

Why DIY Makes Sense Here

Painting rewards patience far more than skill, which is unusual in home improvement. Nothing is structural, nothing is permanent, mistakes sand out or paint over, and you can stop at any point. Replacing $300–$750 of labour with a weekend is the best hourly rate available to most homeowners.

The work that actually determines the result is preparation: moving furniture out, filling and sanding, cleaning dust off the walls, taping edges and laying drop cloths. Expect preparation to take as long as painting. Professionals are faster largely because they prepare efficiently and cut in by hand without tape.

Two tools genuinely worth the money: a quality 2.5-inch angled brush for cutting in, and decent roller covers. Cheap roller covers shed fibres into the finish and no amount of care recovers that.

Scaling Up to a Whole House

Per-room pricing gets cheaper at volume because setup is amortised. Where one bedroom is $350–$900, a whole interior typically runs $2–$6 per square foot of floor area — so a 2,000 sq ft house lands around $4,000–$12,000 for walls, ceilings and trim.

If you are doing it yourself, buying paint in 5-gallon buckets saves 15–25% per gallon over individual cans for colours you are using throughout — typically ceilings, trim and hallways.

One sequencing note that saves real money: paint before flooring and before furniture arrives, if you have the choice. Drop cloths and careful cutting cost time that an empty room simply gives you for free.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to paint a 12x12 room?

Roughly $90-$250 in materials doing it yourself, including two gallons of wall paint, supplies and any primer. Hiring a painter typically costs $350-$900 for walls, with ceiling and trim usually quoted separately.

How many gallons of paint do I need for a room?

About one gallon per 350-400 square feet per coat. A 12x12 room with 8-foot ceilings has roughly 348 square feet of wall after doors and windows, so two gallons covers two coats with a little spare. Add a gallon for the ceiling.

Is it cheaper to paint yourself or hire someone?

Far cheaper to do it yourself - materials are $90-250 against $350-900 for labour on a single room. Painting is low-risk and reversible, which makes it one of the best-value DIY jobs in a house.

Do I need primer before painting?

Primer is needed on bare drywall, over stains or patches, and when covering a dark or saturated colour. Repainting a similar colour over sound, previously painted walls usually does not need it - a quality paint-and-primer product handles that case.
