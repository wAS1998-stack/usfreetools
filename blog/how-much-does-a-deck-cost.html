How Much Does a Deck Cost to Build?

A pressure-treated deck typically runs $25–$40 per square foot installed; composite pushes that to $40–$70. Materials alone are roughly a third of those figures — which is why decking is one of the highest-value DIY projects there is.

Installed Costs by Material

Deck pricing is quoted per square foot of deck surface, and the material choice sets the band:

  • Pressure-treated pine — $25–$40 installed. The default, and still the value leader.
  • Cedar or redwood — $35–$50. Better looking, naturally rot-resistant, needs regular sealing.
  • Composite — $40–$70. No staining ever, 25-year warranties common, highest up-front cost.
  • Tropical hardwood (ipe, cumaru) — $50–$90. Exceptional durability, difficult to work, pre-drilling required for every fastener.

A common 12×16 deck (192 sq ft) therefore spans roughly $4,800 pressure-treated to $13,400 composite installed. That is an enormous range for the same shape of structure, and most of it is decking material plus the labour to install it.

What You Are Actually Paying For

Decking is only part of it. A deck is footings, posts, beams, joists, decking, railings and stairs. Decking boards are typically 25–35% of material cost; framing lumber is another 25–30%; railings can be 15–25% on their own; stairs are disproportionately expensive per square foot because of the stringer work.

Height changes everything. A ground-level deck needs minimal footings, no guard rail in many jurisdictions, and no stairs. Raise it to a second-storey door and you add deep footings, tall posts, bracing, a code-compliant guard rail and a full staircase — often doubling the cost of the same square footage.

Permits and inspection. Most US jurisdictions require a permit for decks above a certain height or attached to the house: typically $150–$500, plus inspection scheduling. Skipping it creates real problems when you sell.

Railings. Pressure-treated wood rail is cheap. Composite rail, aluminium balusters, cable or glass each multiply that line item, and on a small deck the railing can rival the decking cost.

Estimating Your Board Count

Deck boards do not cover their nominal width. A 5/4×6 board is actually 5.5 inches wide, and you leave a gap — typically ⅛ to ¼ inch — so each row consumes about 5.75 inches of deck width.

For a 12-foot-wide deck: 144 inches ÷ 5.75 = 25.04, rounded up to 26 rows. If boards run the 16-foot length, that is 26 boards of 16 feet, or 416 linear feet. At $12–$20 for a 16-foot pressure-treated board, decking alone is $312–$520. The deck board calculator does this and adds the screw count — which is larger than people expect, roughly two per board per joist, so about 676 screws on that deck.

Framing is separate: joists at 16-inch centres, beams, posts and ledger. The board foot calculator converts your cut list into board feet for pricing.

Pressure-Treated vs Composite, Over Time

The composite premium is real but the comparison is not a single moment. A pressure-treated deck needs cleaning and re-sealing every two to three years — call it $150–$400 in materials each time, or $400–$800 if hired out. Over twenty years that is $1,500–$8,000 in maintenance, on top of eventual board replacement as pieces cup, split or rot.

Composite's maintenance is washing. Its failure mode is fading and surface scratching rather than rot, and quality varies a great deal between budget and premium lines — cheap composite can look worse at ten years than well-maintained wood.

The honest framing: if you will genuinely maintain it, pressure-treated is cheaper for a long time. If you know you will not, composite buys you the right to ignore it. Framing stays pressure-treated either way — composite is a surface, not a structure.

Where DIY Saves the Most

Decks are the clearest case in home improvement for doing it yourself: labour is roughly two-thirds of the installed price, and the work is ordinary carpentry rather than a specialised trade. A 192 sq ft pressure-treated deck that bids at $4,800–$7,700 has $1,800–$2,600 of materials in it.

What makes it approachable: there is no clock. Unlike concrete, you can frame one weekend and deck the next. Mistakes are unscrewed and redone. The tools are a circular saw, a drill and a level.

What makes it serious: a deck is a structure that holds people above the ground. Ledger attachment and flashing are the single most common cause of deck collapse, footing depth must suit your frost line, and span tables are not suggestions. Pull the permit, follow the inspector's notes, and have the structural parts checked. The stairs, incidentally, are the fiddly part — the stair calculator handles the rise-and-run arithmetic that trips up most first builds.

Budget Realities

Lumber prices move. Pressure-treated pricing has swung dramatically in recent years. Price your cut list the week you buy, not from a figure you read months earlier.

Hidden structure. Footings, concrete, hardware, joist hangers, flashing and fasteners add up to a surprising share — budget 15–20% beyond the obvious lumber.

Stairs cost more than their footprint. A short flight can equal 30–50 sq ft of deck in time and material.

Buy decking a little long. Boards with bows and splits exist in every stack; ordering 10% over lets you reject the worst instead of installing them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a 12x16 deck cost?

Installed, roughly $4,800-$7,700 in pressure-treated or $7,700-$13,400 in composite, at 192 square feet. Materials alone run about $1,800-$2,600 pressure-treated, which is why building it yourself saves so much on this particular project.

Is composite decking worth the extra cost?

It depends on whether you will actually maintain wood. Composite costs roughly 50-80% more up front but needs only washing, while pressure-treated needs re-sealing every two to three years. If that maintenance will not happen, composite is cheaper in practice.

How many deck boards do I need?

Divide the deck width by the board width plus the gap. A 5/4x6 board is 5.5 inches wide, so with a quarter-inch gap each row uses 5.75 inches - a 12-foot-wide deck needs 26 rows. Add 5-10% for cuts and culling bad boards.

Do I need a permit to build a deck?

Usually yes if the deck is attached to the house or raised above a certain height, which varies by jurisdiction. Permits typically run $150-500 and bring inspections that catch ledger and footing problems - the two failures that actually hurt people.
