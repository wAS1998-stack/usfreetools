How Baker’s Percentages Work

In baker's math, flour is always 100% and every other ingredient is expressed as a percentage of the flour's weight. A 65% hydration dough with 500 g of flour therefore takes 325 g of water; 2% salt is 10 g; 0.3% instant yeast is 1.5 g. This convention is what makes recipes portable and scalable — the percentages describe the dough itself, independent of batch size, so doubling the flour doubles everything while the dough behaves identically.

Hydration is the percentage that most shapes the result. Lower hydration (55-60%) gives a firm, easy-to-shape dough and a denser, crisp crust. Mid hydration (60-65%) is the sweet spot for most home pizza — workable dough, good oven spring, classic texture. High hydration (70%+) produces the open, blistered crumb of pan pizzas and artisan styles, at the cost of a sticky dough that demands folding technique rather than kneading. The calculator lets you pick the number deliberately instead of discovering it by accident.

Dialing In Your Dough

Start where your style lives: around 60-62% for a first Neapolitan-style attempt, 65% for New York slices, 70-75% for pan pizza or focaccia. Weigh everything — flour, water, salt, yeast — because grams are the whole point; a digital kitchen scale costing a few dollars is the single biggest upgrade to home pizza. Mix, rest, and if the dough feels wrong, change one variable next time: hydration up for a lighter crumb, down for easier handling. One change at a time is how you converge on your dough.

Salt and yeast percentages tune flavor and timing. Salt at 2-3% seasons the dough and tightens the gluten; going far below 2% tastes flat. Yeast quantity sets fermentation speed: around 0.4-0.5% instant yeast for same-day dough, down to 0.1-0.2% for a slow 24-72 hour cold ferment in the fridge — which is where serious flavor develops. Because every figure is anchored to your flour weight, the formula this calculator gives you scales perfectly from a single test ball to a party batch, with the same dough coming out every time.

Flour Choice and Fermentation: The Other Half of Great Dough

Hydration behaves differently depending on the flour, because protein content determines how much water the dough can hold gracefully. Bread flour (roughly 12–14% protein) builds the strong gluten network that high hydration demands, which is why it is the default for pizza; all-purpose (10–12%) works well up to the mid-60s hydration; and true Italian 00 flour, finely milled, produces the silky extensible dough of Neapolitan tradition, typically in the 58–65% range. If a recipe's hydration feels unmanageable, the flour may simply be weaker than the recipe assumed — either drop the hydration a few points or step up to a stronger flour rather than fighting a slack dough.

Fermentation is where flavor actually comes from, and yeast percentage is its throttle. A same-day dough with 0.4–0.5% instant yeast rises in hours but tastes plain; dropping to 0.1–0.2% and cold-fermenting in the refrigerator for 24–72 hours develops the complex, slightly tangy depth that good pizzerias have — time doing the work that yeast quantity otherwise rushes. The practical schedule: mix dough tonight with low yeast, ball it, refrigerate, and bake tomorrow or the day after, pulling it out an hour or two ahead to warm. Combined with the exact water, salt, and yeast this calculator gives you, that one habit — planning a day ahead — is the single biggest jump in homemade pizza quality available without buying an oven.

Quick Tips

  • Flour is always 100%; water, salt, and yeast are percentages of the flour weight.
  • 60-65% hydration is the easiest starting range for home pizza.
  • Use about 2-3% salt; below 2% the dough tastes flat.
  • Less yeast + a long cold ferment in the fridge = far more flavor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is dough hydration?

Hydration is the weight of water as a percentage of the flour weight — baker's percentage. 650 g of water with 1000 g of flour is 65% hydration. Higher hydration gives a wetter dough, an airier crumb, and a crispier crust, but is harder to handle.

What hydration should pizza dough be?

Classic Neapolitan sits around 58-65%, New York style about 60-65%, and pan or focaccia-style pizzas run 70-80%. Beginners find 60-65% easiest to handle; very high hydration doughs are sticky and need practice.

How much salt and yeast should I use?

Common baker's percentages are about 2-3% salt and roughly 0.2-0.5% instant yeast (more for same-day dough, less for long cold ferments). The calculator applies typical values you can adjust to your recipe.

Why measure dough ingredients by weight?

Because volume is wildly inconsistent for flour — a cup can vary by 20% or more depending on packing. Baker's percentages only work with weight, and weighing is what makes a dough recipe exactly repeatable.
