🏠 Roof Pitch Calculator

Enter the rise per 12 inches of run to get the roof’s angle in degrees and its pitch factor — the multiplier that turns footprint into true roof area.

What This Calculator Does

This calculator translates roof pitch between the three languages it is spoken in: the carpenter's rise-over-12 notation (like 6/12), the angle in degrees, and the pitch factor — the multiplier that converts a house's footprint into the true, sloped roof area that materials must cover. Enter the rise and all three appear.

Pitch runs through every roofing decision: what materials are allowed, whether the surface is walkable, how much shingle to order, and how the roof sheds weather. One number, three notations, and this tool keeps them straight.

Rise Over Run, Degrees, and the Trig Between

US roofing quotes slope as inches of rise per 12 inches of horizontal run: a 4/12 roof climbs 4 inches per foot, a 12/12 climbs a foot per foot. The degree conversion is one arctangent — angle = atan(rise/12) — giving the familiar ladder: 4/12 ≈ 18.4°, 6/12 ≈ 26.6°, 8/12 ≈ 33.7°, 12/12 = 45°. Measuring an existing roof is easiest with a level and tape from inside the attic (level held horizontal against a rafter, measure the rise at the 12-inch mark) or a phone inclinometer on the rake edge.

The pitch factor is the practical payoff of the trigonometry: √(rise² + 144) ÷ 12, the ratio of sloped length to horizontal run. It is how estimators go from a footprint they can measure (or read off a plan) to the true surface: factor 1.054 at 4/12, 1.118 at 6/12, 1.202 at 8/12, 1.414 at 12/12. A 1,500 sq ft footprint under a 8/12 roof carries about 1,803 sq ft of actual roofing — a 20% difference the footprint alone hides, and exactly the kind of gap that strands a re-roof three squares short.

Pitch and What It Permits

Materials carry minimum pitches because slope is the roof's first defense: water must run off faster than it can wick under laps. Standard asphalt shingles are rated down to 2/12 (with enhanced underlayment between 2/12 and 4/12); below that, membranes and specialized low-slope systems take over — shingling a 1/12 porch roof is a leak on a schedule. Metal panels reach lower (commonly 3/12, some standing-seam to 1/4:12), tile and slate want moderate slopes and engineering for their weight, and flat-roof membranes own everything near horizontal.

Slope also sets the working conditions. Up to about 6/12 an experienced person can walk a dry roof with care; 7/12 through 9/12 is roof-jack and toe-board territory; steeper belongs to harnesses, staging, and usually professionals — a fact worth pricing into any DIY ambition, since fall protection is not where budgets should economize. Weather performance runs the other way: steeper roofs shed rain and especially snow better, which is why heavy-snow regions build steep and dry regions tolerate low slopes. Pitch, in short, is a regional dialect with engineering behind it.

Using the Factor for Real Estimates

The estimating workflow: measure the building's footprint under roof (including overhangs — eaves extend past the walls), multiply by the pitch factor for sloped area, and convert to roofing squares at 100 sq ft per square. The 1,500 sq ft footprint at 8/12 becomes 1,803 sq ft ≈ 18 squares, before the waste margin (10% for simple gables; 15%+ for hips, valleys, and dormers, whose cuts multiply). Complex roofs decompose into rectangles per plane, each times the factor, exactly like every area problem on this site.

The same factor prices everything sloped: underlayment, sheathing on new construction, even gutter runs stay horizontal but rafter lengths grow by the factor (a 12-ft horizontal run under 8/12 pitch needs a 14.4-ft rafter before tails). Solar installers, painters bidding steep gables, and anyone renting a lift all consult the pitch first. It is a genuinely small piece of trigonometry with outsized reach — and having the three notations converted instantly is what keeps estimates, orders, and contractor conversations speaking the same number.

Quick Tips

  • Pitch = rise per 12″ run; angle = atan(rise/12); 12/12 = 45°.
  • Pitch factor √(rise²+144)/12 turns footprint into true roof area.
  • Asphalt shingles need ≥2/12 (enhanced underlayment 2-4/12); lower slopes need membranes.
  • Walkable to ~6/12; add 10% waste for gables, 15%+ for hips and valleys.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a 6/12 roof pitch mean?

The roof rises 6 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal run. That is an angle of about 26.6 degrees — a common, moderately sloped US residential roof. Pitch is always quoted as rise over a 12-inch run.

How do I convert roof pitch to degrees?

Angle = arctangent(rise ÷ 12). A 4/12 pitch is 18.4°, 6/12 is 26.6°, 9/12 is 36.9°, and 12/12 is exactly 45°. The calculator does the trigonometry instantly.

What is the pitch factor?

The multiplier converting a roof's horizontal footprint to its true sloped area: √(rise² + 12²) ÷ 12. A 6/12 roof has a factor of 1.118 — a 1,000 sq ft footprint carries about 1,118 sq ft of actual roof, which is what shingles must cover.

What roof pitch is walkable?

Roughly 6/12 and below is generally considered walkable with care; 7/12-9/12 requires roof jacks and experience; above that is harness-and-staging territory. Steeper also means better shedding of rain and snow — the tradeoff that sets regional styles.
