What This Calculator Does

This calculator works with aspect ratios — the proportional relationship between width and height. Enter your original dimensions and either a new width or a new height, and it instantly calculates the missing dimension so the shape stays exactly the same. It also shows the simplified ratio (like 16:9) of any dimensions you enter.

Keeping the aspect ratio is what stops images and videos from looking stretched or squashed when you resize them. Doing the proportional math by hand invites rounding mistakes; this calculator does it precisely, which is why an aspect ratio calculator is a staple for anyone resizing images, editing video, designing screens, or preparing content for different platforms.

How Aspect Ratios Work

An aspect ratio expresses shape independent of size: 1920×1080, 1280×720, and 640×360 are different sizes but all 16:9, so they look identical in proportion. The ratio is found by dividing both dimensions by their greatest common divisor — the largest number that divides both evenly. This is why resizing within the same ratio never distorts: every dimension pair on that ratio is just the same shape at a different scale.

Solving for a missing dimension is simple proportion math: new height equals new width multiplied by the original height-to-width relationship. The arithmetic is easy to state and easy to fumble — especially with awkward numbers where rounding one side throws the proportions subtly off, producing images that look almost right but slightly wrong. The calculator applies the proportion exactly and rounds sensibly, so the result preserves the shape as closely as whole pixels allow.

Where Aspect Ratios Matter

Images and video are the everyday cases. Resizing a photo for a website, scaling a thumbnail, exporting video for a platform, or fitting an image into a layout all require preserving the ratio, or the content visibly distorts — faces widen, circles become ovals. Different platforms also expect different ratios: 16:9 for standard video, 9:16 for vertical phone video, 1:1 squares for many profile images, and various banner shapes. Knowing the target ratio and calculating exact pixel dimensions for it is a constant small task in content work.

Design and screens rely on the same math. Screen resolutions are defined by ratios (most modern displays are 16:9), print and photography have their own conventions like 3:2, and responsive design constantly scales elements while preserving proportion. Whether you are cropping a photo to fit a frame, choosing a resolution, sizing a video embed, or preparing one image for several platforms, the underlying question is the same — “what dimensions keep this shape?” — and that is exactly what this calculator answers instantly.

Common Aspect Ratios and What They Are For

A handful of ratios cover most of what you will meet. 16:9 (1920×1080, 3840×2160) is the standard for video, TVs, monitors, and presentation slides — the default shape of the modern screen. 4:3 (1024×768) is the older, squarer standard, still common in tablets, document scans, and legacy content. 1:1 squares dominate profile pictures and certain social feeds. 9:16 is vertical video — phone-first formats like stories and shorts — which is simply 16:9 rotated. Photography leans on 3:2 (the classic 35mm frame, and most DSLR sensors) and 4:5 for portrait-oriented prints and posts, while cinema uses wider shapes like 21:9. Knowing the target ratio before you shoot, design, or export saves the painful discovery that your composition does not fit the destination.

When content and container ratios differ, something has to give, and there are only three honest options: letterbox (fit inside, accepting bars), crop (fill the frame, losing edges), or stretch (fill by distorting — almost always the wrong answer). This is why a 16:9 video on a 9:16 phone screen shows bars or gets cropped to its center, and why preparing platform-specific versions beats exporting one shape everywhere. The calculator supports that workflow directly: start from your master dimensions, compute the exact pixel sizes for each target ratio, and crop deliberately rather than letting a platform's automatic crop choose for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an aspect ratio?

It is the proportional relationship between width and height, written like 16:9 or 4:3. Two sizes with the same aspect ratio have the same shape — one is just a scaled version of the other — which is why keeping the ratio prevents stretching or squashing.

How do I resize an image without distorting it?

Keep the new dimensions in the same aspect ratio as the original. Enter the original width and height plus your new width, and the calculator gives the exact height that preserves the shape (or vice versa).

What are the most common aspect ratios?

16:9 dominates video, TVs, and monitors; 4:3 is the older screen standard; 1:1 is square (common for profile images); 9:16 is vertical video for phones; and 3:2 is common in photography.

How is the simplified ratio worked out?

The calculator divides the width and height by their greatest common divisor. So 1920×1080 divides by 120 to give 16:9 — the smallest whole numbers that keep the same proportions.
