🌱 Mulch Calculator

Enter your bed area and mulch depth to see exactly how much to buy — in cubic feet, cubic yards, and standard 2 cu ft bags.

What This Calculator Does

This calculator tells you how much mulch to buy. Enter the bed's area in square feet and the depth you want in inches, and it returns the volume in cubic feet, cubic yards (how bulk mulch is sold), and the number of standard 2-cubic-foot bags. It converts a landscaping guess into a shopping list.

Mulch is one of those purchases people reliably get wrong — usually under, sometimes wildly over — because volume in three dimensions defies eyeballing. The calculation is simple once written down, and doing it before the garden-center trip means one trip, the right amount, and beds that get the full intended depth.

The Volume Math Behind Mulch

Mulch quantity is volume: area times depth. Measure the bed's square footage (length times width for rectangles; split irregular beds into simple shapes and add them). Convert the depth from inches to feet by dividing by 12, then multiply: a 100 sq ft bed at 3 inches is 100 × 0.25 = 25 cubic feet. Bulk mulch sells by the cubic yard — 27 cubic feet — so that bed needs 25/27 ≈ 0.93 yards. In bags, the common 2 cu ft size means about 13 bags for the same job.

The coverage shortcut runs the same math backward: one cubic yard spread at 3 inches covers about 108 square feet, at 2 inches about 162, and at 1 inch the often-quoted 324. Those figures let you sanity-check a quote or an estimate instantly — if a supplier suggests 5 yards for a 200 sq ft bed at 3 inches, something is off by more than double. The calculator does the precise version of both directions, so the number you order matches the beds you actually have.

Choosing Depth and Applying It Well

Depth is a horticultural decision, not just a volume input. The working range is 2-4 inches: enough to suppress weeds, retain soil moisture, and moderate temperature, without smothering roots. Fine mulches (shredded leaves, compost, fine bark) pack denser and work at about 2 inches; coarse bark nuggets need 3-4 to close the light gaps weeds exploit. Beyond 4 inches, mulch holds excessive moisture, can turn hydrophobic and shed water, and invites rot — more is genuinely worse.

Application technique protects the plants the mulch is meant to help. Keep mulch pulled back a few inches from trunks and stems — the notorious “mulch volcano” piled against a tree traps moisture on bark and invites disease and girdling roots; a flat donut with a clear center is correct. Refresh annually rather than rebuilding: existing mulch decomposes into the soil (a feature, not a flaw), so most years need only an inch or so of top-up, which the calculator prices out just as easily as a full application. Water beds before mulching, weed first, and the layer you calculated does its job for the full season.

Bulk Versus Bags: Buying Smart

The bulk-versus-bags decision usually turns on quantity and access. Bulk mulch by the cubic yard typically costs meaningfully less per unit than bagged once you pass a yard or two, and a delivery saves a dozen car trips — but it arrives as a pile on the driveway that must be wheelbarrowed while fresh. Bags cost more per cubic foot but stack, store, stay dry, carry to backyards through narrow gates, and let you spread the job across weekends. Many gardeners split the difference: bulk for the big front beds, a few bags for touch-ups and awkward corners.

Two buying notes save disappointment. First, bag sizes vary — 2 cu ft is the common standard this calculator assumes, but 1.5 and 3 cu ft bags exist, so check the bag and adjust the count proportionally. Second, ordering bulk slightly over (rounding 2.7 yards to 3) is usually wiser than under, since the leftover mulches a path or the compost area, while a shortfall means a second delivery fee. Color-dyed, natural bark, cedar, and rubber mulches all measure identically — the volume math is universal even when the material choice is a matter of taste and purpose.

Quick Tips

  • Volume = area × depth (inches ÷ 12); one cubic yard = 27 cu ft ≈ 13.5 two-cu-ft bags.
  • Use 2 inches for fine mulch, 3-4 for coarse bark; never exceed 4.
  • Keep mulch a few inches off trunks — no mulch volcanoes.
  • One yard at 3 inches covers ~108 sq ft; round bulk orders slightly up.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much mulch do I need?

Multiply the bed area (square feet) by the depth in feet (inches / 12), giving cubic feet; divide by 27 for cubic yards. A 100 sq ft bed at 3 inches needs 25 cu ft — about 0.93 cubic yards, or roughly 13 two-cubic-foot bags.

How deep should mulch be?

2-4 inches is the standard range: about 2 inches for fine mulches, 3-4 for coarse bark. Less than 2 inches will not suppress weeds well; more than 4 can suffocate roots and hold excess moisture against stems.

Should I buy bags or bulk?

Roughly, one cubic yard equals 13.5 of the common 2 cu ft bags. Bulk delivery is usually cheaper past about a yard or two if you can receive a pile; bags win for small jobs, tight access, and spreading over time.

How much does a cubic yard of mulch cover?

At 3 inches deep, one cubic yard covers about 108 square feet (324 sq ft at 1 inch). This coverage rule is handy for quick estimates before the precise calculation.
