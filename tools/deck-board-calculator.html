🛖 Deck Board Calculator

Enter your deck’s dimensions and board details to see how many boards to buy — with the gap spacing and fastener math handled for you.

What This Calculator Does

This calculator counts decking. Enter the deck's length and width, the board width and the gap you will leave, and it returns how many boards of your chosen length the surface needs, plus the approximate screw count. It converts the lumberyard question — “how many 16-footers?” — into a number you can order.

Deck boards are bought in fixed lengths and installed with deliberate gaps, which makes the count less obvious than plain area math. The calculator handles the effective-width arithmetic that decking actually follows, so the order matches the deck.

Effective Width: The Decking Arithmetic

Boards cover their actual width plus the gap beside them. The standard 5/4×6 decking board measures 5.5 inches across (nominal names overstate lumber — a “2×6” is also 5.5 wide); add a 1/4-inch gap and each row consumes 5.75 inches of deck width. Rows = deck width ÷ effective width, rounded up: a 12-ft-wide deck is 144 inches ÷ 5.75 ≈ 25.04 → 26 rows. If boards run the deck's 16-ft length in single spans, that is 26 boards of 16 ft.

When the deck is longer than available boards, rows are built from butted segments — a 20-ft run might use a 12 and an 8 — and joints must land on joists, staggered between neighboring rows so they do not stripe the deck. In that case count total linear feet (rows × deck length) and divide by the board length you are buying, then round up per row segmenting. The calculator reports both rows and total linear footage so either purchasing style works.

Gaps, Materials, and Movement

The gap is functional, not decorative: it drains rain, sheds debris, and gives boards room to move with moisture. The right gap at install depends on the material's moisture state. Kiln-dried wood and composite decking are installed at their final gap — 1/8 to 1/4 inch, per the manufacturer (composites specify exactly, and their warranties care). Wet pressure-treated lumber, the classic green-brown budget board, is commonly installed nearly tight because it shrinks as it dries, opening its own gap over the first season; gapping wet PT a full 1/4 inch can yield 1/2-inch canyons by next summer.

Movement also drives fastening. Screws (or hidden clips for grooved composite) beat nails, which back out as boards cycle; two fasteners per board per joist is standard, so a 26-board deck over joists at 16-inch centers — 13 joists across 16 ft — wants roughly 26 × 13 × 2 ≈ 676 screws, which is why decking screws sell in 5-lb boxes. Composite and hardwood decking each have their own fastener systems; whatever the system, the count scales the same way and the calculator's estimate keeps the order honest.

Patterns, Waste, and Buying Well

Straight decking perpendicular to the joists is the efficient default: 5-10% waste covers end cuts and culls. Diagonal decking looks sharp and costs for it — every board ends in an angled cut whose offcut rarely reuses, pushing waste to 15%+, and it shortens effective spans (diagonal boards cross joists at a longer interval, sometimes requiring tighter joist spacing). Picture-frame borders — a perimeter board framing the field — add their own linear footage and mitered corners, plus blocking beneath. The pattern decision is aesthetic; its waste percentage is arithmetic, and it belongs in the order.

At the yard, the extra margin buys selection power: lumber stacks contain crowned, bowed, and wane-edged boards, and ordering 10% over lets you reject the worst rather than install them. Check boards for crown and lay them crown-up; keep composite of one production run together for color; and store boards flat and stickered if installation waits. A deck surface is the most visible lumber purchase most people make — the calculator gets the quantity right, and the margin gets the quality right.

Quick Tips

  • Rows = deck width ÷ (board width + gap); 5/4×6 boards are 5.5″ wide.
  • Gap kiln-dried and composite at 1/8-1/4″; wet PT installs tight and shrinks open.
  • Two screws per board per joist — decks consume screws by the pound.
  • Buy 5-10% extra (15%+ for diagonal) and use it to cull bad boards.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many deck boards do I need?

Divide the deck's width by the board's effective width (actual width plus the gap). A standard 5/4x6 board is 5.5 inches wide; with a 1/4-inch gap that is 5.75 inches per row. A 12-ft-wide deck needs 12 x 12 ÷ 5.75 ≈ 26 rows of boards running its length.

What gap should I leave between deck boards?

About 1/8 to 1/4 inch for drainage and debris clearance. Kiln-dried and composite boards are gapped at install; wet pressure-treated lumber is often installed tight because it shrinks a gap open as it dries — check the manufacturer's guidance.

What is a 5/4x6 deck board actually?

Nominal naming: a '5/4x6' measures about 1 x 5.5 inches after drying and surfacing, and a '2x6' about 1.5 x 5.5. Board math must use the actual 5.5-inch width, not the name.

How much extra decking should I buy?

5-10% for straight decking (cut waste and culling bowed boards), 15%+ for diagonal or picture-frame patterns. Buying the extra up front also lets you reject the worst boards in the stack — a real advantage with lumber.
