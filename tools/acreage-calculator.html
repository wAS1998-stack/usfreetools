🌾 Acreage Calculator

Enter a parcel’s length and width in feet to get its area in acres, square feet, and hectares — the land units that never quite feel intuitive.

What This Calculator Does

This calculator turns land dimensions into acreage. Enter a parcel's length and width in feet and it returns the area in square feet, acres, and hectares. It answers the questions land listings and property projects constantly raise: how many acres is this lot, what does 0.3 acres mean in feet, and how do these figures translate to the metric hectares the rest of the world uses.

Land units are uniquely unintuitive — nobody has a feel for 43,560 of anything — so buyers, sellers, and owners routinely misjudge parcel sizes by large factors. Converting to and from acres precisely, with a visual anchor or two, replaces that fog with numbers you can compare and act on.

The Acre, Decoded

An acre is 43,560 square feet, and the odd number has a tidy origin: it is one chain (66 feet) by one furlong (660 feet) — medieval plowing units that survived into modern land law. Any parcel's acreage is simply its square footage divided by 43,560: a 100 × 150 ft lot is 15,000 sq ft, or 0.344 acres. The default dimensions in this calculator, 660 × 66, produce exactly one acre — the historical strip itself.

Visual anchors make the unit usable. A square acre is about 209 feet per side. An American football field between the goal lines (300 × 160 ft) is 48,000 sq ft — a bit over an acre — making the common “roughly a football field” shorthand honest. Suburban lots cluster around a quarter acre (10,890 sq ft); a standard city block often encloses several acres. With those references, a listing's “0.68 acres” becomes a picture rather than a mystery number, which is precisely the intuition land decisions need.

Acres, Hectares, and When Each Appears

The hectare is the metric counterpart: a 100 × 100 meter square, equal to about 2.47 acres (one acre is 0.4047 hectares). US real estate, zoning, and agriculture speak acres; international listings, science, and most of the world's land administration speak hectares — so cross-border property research, agricultural data, and environmental reporting constantly demand the conversion this calculator performs. Larger still, 640 acres make a square mile (a “section” in the US survey system), the grid that explains the checkerboard geometry of the American Midwest from any airplane window.

Context determines the useful unit. Comparing two US listings: acres, directly. Reading a farm's yield data or a conservation report: hectares are likely native. Fencing, seeding, or irrigating: square feet feed the materials math better than either. Because the calculator shows all three simultaneously, whichever direction the question arrives from — feet to acres, acres to hectares, or dimensions to everything — the answer is on screen without chaining conversions by hand, where factor errors of 10× are notoriously easy.

Estimating Real Parcels Accurately

Rectangular lots are a single multiplication, but real parcels wander. The reliable technique is decomposition: divide the shape into rectangles and triangles (a triangle is half its base times height), compute each in square feet, and sum before dividing by 43,560. Pacing a boundary gives rough dimensions — an adult pace runs near 2.5 feet — while a measuring wheel, a long tape, or the measure tools in online mapping services give far better ones. For sloped land, note that legal area is the horizontal (map) projection, not the surface along the slope.

Know when estimation ends and documentation begins. For purchases, disputes, subdivision, and anything legal, the recorded plat and a licensed surveyor's figure are the authority — an estimate is for understanding, not for boundaries. Listings occasionally round generously, and older deeds can disagree with modern surveys, which is itself worth discovering early. Used in that spirit, this calculator handles the honest middle ground: turning dimensions you can measure into acreage you can compare, budget around, and question intelligently when the paperwork tells a different story.

Quick Tips

  • 1 acre = 43,560 sq ft — a square about 209 ft per side, near a football field.
  • 1 acre ≈ 0.4047 hectares; 1 hectare ≈ 2.47 acres; 640 acres = 1 sq mile.
  • Split irregular lots into rectangles and triangles, then sum.
  • For legal boundaries, the plat and surveyor govern — estimates are for understanding.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many square feet are in an acre?

43,560. The historical acre is one furlong by one chain — 660 by 66 feet — and that product is exactly 43,560 sq ft. Divide any parcel's square footage by 43,560 to get acres.

How big is an acre visually?

About 90% of an American football field between the goal lines, or a square roughly 209 feet per side. A typical suburban quarter-acre lot is about 10,890 sq ft.

How do acres convert to hectares?

One acre is about 0.4047 hectares; one hectare is about 2.47 acres. Hectares are the metric land unit (a 100 x 100 m square), standard outside the US and in scientific use.

How do I find the acreage of an irregular lot?

Split it into rectangles and triangles, compute each area in square feet, sum them, and divide by 43,560. For legal purposes, the recorded plat or a surveyor's figure governs over any estimate.
