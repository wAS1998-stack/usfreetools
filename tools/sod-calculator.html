🟩 Sod Calculator

Enter your lawn area to see the sod you need — in standard rolls and pallets — with the waste margin and weight numbers that make delivery day go smoothly.

What This Calculator Does

This calculator converts a lawn area into a sod order. Enter the square footage and it returns the number of standard rolls and the pallet count, with the waste margin applied. It also grounds the two logistics numbers that surprise first-time buyers: what the order weighs, and why it must go down the day it arrives.

Sod is the instant lawn — and a perishable, heavy one. The difference between a smooth install and a stressful one is almost entirely planning arithmetic: the right quantity, delivered to the right spot, on the morning you are ready. That is what this page sets up.

Rolls, Pallets, and the Order Math

The US standard sod roll covers about 10 sq ft — typically a 2 × 5 ft slab rolled up — so the roll count is simply area ÷ 10 after adding a 5-10% waste margin for the cuts along beds, walks, and curves. A 2,000 sq ft lawn at 10% waste needs 2,200 sq ft → 220 rolls. Pallets bundle roughly 450 sq ft (farms range 400-500 — confirm yours when ordering), putting that lawn at about 5 pallets. Farms sell by the pallet or by total square footage; either way the area drives it.

Measure the actual lawn shape rather than eyeballing: decompose the yard into rectangles and triangles, subtract beds, walks, and driveway, and sum — the same decomposition as any area job, and worth doing carefully because sod's per-square-foot cost makes overestimates expensive and underestimates mean visible seams where a later patch never quite matches. The waste margin covers honest trimming, not measurement error.

Delivery Day: Weight and the Clock

Sod logistics are governed by two facts. First, weight: rolls run 30-45 lbs each depending on soil moisture, and a pallet weighs 1,500-3,000 lbs — far beyond moving by hand. Delivery trucks place pallets with a forklift or piggyback loader, and where they set them is where they stay, so choose drop points that shorten the wheelbarrow runs and spread multiple pallets around large yards rather than clustering them.

Second, the clock: sod is living grass harvested with an inch of soil, and stacked rolls compost themselves — internal heat builds within hours, yellowing the grass in a day or two and killing it faster in summer heat. The professional rhythm is: finish all ground prep first (grade, amend, rake, lightly firm), schedule delivery for the morning of installation, and lay the same day, watering sections as they go down rather than waiting for the end. An order sized by this calculator, staged by this rhythm, goes from truck to green lawn in one satisfying day — which is precisely sod's selling point.

Sod or Seed: The Honest Comparison

Sod's advantages are speed and certainty: usable lawn in two to three weeks, immediate erosion control on slopes, near-year-round installation windows, and a dense mat that gives weeds no opening. Its costs are the honest counterweight — several times seed's price per square foot, less variety (you take the farm's blend), and the perishability logistics above. Seed wins on price, species choice, and deep root establishment, but demands the right season, weeks of consistent moisture, and tolerance for a patchy adolescence that sod skips entirely.

The practical split most landscapers use: sod for front yards where appearance matters now, slopes that would wash out under seed, and projects with deadlines (selling a house, an event); seed for large areas where sod's cost multiplies painfully, and for renovators who want a specific grass variety. Hybrid approaches work too — sod the visible front, seed the back. Whichever route, the aftercare weeks are the same: daily watering at first, no traffic until rooted (tug a corner; resistance means roots), and a first mow when it reaches normal mowing height. Quick Tips

  • Rolls ≈ (area + 5-10%) ÷ 10 sq ft; pallets ≈ area ÷ 450 (confirm your farm's size).
  • Pallets weigh 1,500-3,000 lbs — plan drop spots; they do not move twice.
  • Prep first, deliver in the morning, lay the same day, water as you go.
  • Tug-test for rooting before resuming traffic and normal mowing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much sod do I need?

Measure the area in square feet and add about 5-10% for cuts and edges. Standard US sod rolls cover about 10 sq ft (2 x 5 ft), and a pallet typically carries 450 sq ft (400-500 by farm). A 2,000 sq ft lawn is about 200 rolls — roughly 4.5 pallets.

How much does sod weigh?

A single roll runs 30-45 lbs depending on moisture, and a pallet weighs 1,500-3,000 lbs. That is why pallets ship by truck with a forklift, and why placement of the delivery matters — pallets do not move twice.

How fast must sod be installed?

Same day, ideally within 24 hours of harvest. Sod is living turf; stacked rolls heat up and yellow within a day or two (faster in summer). Schedule delivery for the morning of installation, with the ground prepped in advance.

Is sod better than seeding?

Sod buys instant, weed-resistant coverage and works nearly year-round, at several times the material cost of seed. Seed is far cheaper and offers more variety but needs weeks of babying and the right season. Slopes and high-traffic deadlines favor sod; budgets and patience favor seed.
